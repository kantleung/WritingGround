# Bolivia, 1985

The prototype that was not a transition. Use this file for section III of the outline, and for the syllogism the later chapters cash out.

## What was fetched

Morales and Sachs, "Ending the Hyperinflation, 1985–88," NBER / University of Chicago Press, 1990. Chapter pages 238–251. https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c8939/c8939.pdf

Wikipedia, "Supreme Decree 21060." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Decree_21060 — secondary; marked **wiki** below.

## Facts safe to use

- President Hernán Siles Zuazo was compelled to call elections for July 1985, a year ahead of schedule. A last stabilisation attempt in February 1985 prompted a march on La Paz and a month-long sit-in by 10,000 miners. The government capitulated, then gave up even the pretence of stabilising before a new government arrived in August.
- Víctor Paz Estenssoro's New Economic Policy was unveiled on 29 August 1985, three weeks after he took office. Within days, the hyperinflation ended.
- The end was not quite a straight line. Later in 1985, after several weeks of low inflation, prices jumped again, associated with a large emission of money at the end of the year. That blip was brought under control in mid-January 1986. Useful against the cartoon of a single Monday on which inflation dies forever.
- The programme was not stabilisation alone. Morales and Sachs say it embraced widespread liberalisation of trade and finance as well as fiscal austerity. The Bolivian government believed, "with considerable reason," that the liberalisation measures helped the stabilisation take hold. That belief is part of the intellectual path to Warsaw: the dose was already being bundled.
- Stabilisation elements in their account: a unified exchange rate, without capital or exchange controls, initially floated (with a ceiling on peso appreciation), then a dirty float; an immediate cut in the fiscal deficit by raising public-sector prices, especially petroleum, and by freezing public-sector wages.
- First-year measures, from their table of the New Economic Policy: target deficit of 6.3 per cent of GNP, of which 5.3 per cent to be financed from abroad; tariffs unified to a flat 20 per cent; trade quotas and non-tariff barriers eliminated; most price controls eliminated, exceptions including public transport and public utilities; restrictions on private wages removed except for a national minimum wage; restrictions on hiring and firing removed.

## Wiki, check before using

- The decree is Supreme Decree 21060, promulgated 29 August 1985. The date agrees with Morales and Sachs. The decree number is **wiki**; it is standard, but the NBER chapter as fetched says "New Economic Policy," not the decree number.
- Jeffrey Sachs advised the government, and Bolivia was the first country in which he could try the stabilisation argument. **wiki** for the "first country" phrasing. Consistent with his later career, but the fetched NBER chapter is co-authored history, not a statement that he designed the decree.
- The IMF approved the decree and extended $57 million in credit; the World Bank resumed lending. **wiki.** Do not use the sum until an IMF source confirms it.
- The peso was devalued 93 per cent overnight, "in fact installing the US dollar as currency." **wiki**, and over-sharp. Morales and Sachs describe a unified, floating, then managed rate and free convertibility, not dollarisation by decree. Prefer their wording.

## What this file does not contain

A peak inflation figure. The fetched pages say the hyperinflation ended within days. They do not give the annual or monthly peak. Leave the draft at "hyperinflation of a severity that made the monthly rate the only number that mattered" until Sachs's own series is open.

## The distinction to protect

Bolivia in 1985 already had markets, private owners, and prices — mad prices, but prices. The disease was a fiscal deficit financed by the printing press. Ending that is not the same operation as converting a planned economy. The false syllogism, to be stated in the article and not in this note as a fact: if a sudden fiscal and monetary break can end hyperinflation, a sudden break can found a market economy.
