# The term, the ancestors, and what was not read

Section I, section II, and the theoretical core. Most of this file is a record of what the outline asserts from the standard literature, and of the one fetched page that bears on the definition. It is here so a later draft does not confuse "known to the writer" with "checked this session."

## The definition, as the encyclopedia states it

Wikipedia, "Shock therapy (economics)," opening, fetched twice and truncated both times. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_therapy_(economics)

The page defines shock therapy as a group of policies meant to be implemented simultaneously: liberalisation of all prices, privatisation, trade liberalisation, and stabilisation by tight monetary and fiscal policy. In post-communist states, the aim was the passage from plan to market.

A second paragraph on the same page widens the term to any sudden neoliberal reform of a mixed, planned, or developmentalist economy, and says the policies "can be distilled to price liberalisation accompanied by strict austerity." That distillation is exactly what the article must refuse. It drops privatisation in one sentence and smuggles it back in the next, and it equates a stabilisation with a change of owners.

The page calls the neoliberal reforms of Chile under Pinochet the first instance. The outline resists this. Chile in 1975 is an ancestor: an inflation shock recommended under dictatorship, followed by a structural programme and, in 1982, a banking crisis. It was not the conversion of a Soviet-type planned economy. The fetched text breaks off at the Chile claim. The rest of the page was not read. Do not cite its later sections.

Balcerowicz's own 2000 article does not use the phrase. He says Economic Transformation Program. The advocates' preferred words, in the standard record, were radical reform and big bang. "Shock therapy" is the name the public argument kept. That last sentence is the outline's judgement, not a quotation from a fetched page.

## Ancestors

Not fetched this session. Do not write scene-level detail as if a source were open.

- West German currency reform, June 1948. Ludwig Erhard, the Deutsche Mark, the lifting of price controls. The anecdote in which he tells the occupiers he has not relaxed the controls but abolished them is the standard telling. Label it as such, or cut it, until a primary or a serious history is open.
- Chile, 1975. Friedman's visit and the recommendation of a shock against inflation; the Chicago Boys' longer programme; the 1982 banking crisis. Dates of the visit and of the decree should be checked before the draft is specific. The analytical point does not depend on the week: force, an already market economy, and a later financial crisis.

Two cautions for the theoretical chapter.

Blanchard and Kremer wrote after the collapse they explain. The article may use disorganisation as the best later account of a mechanism. It must not imply the mechanism was the reformers' own prediction in 1989.

The fairness sentence in the outline — radicals underweighted institutions and coordination; gradualists underweighted hyperinflation, the closing window, and plunder during a long transition — is the article's judgement. It is not a finding in any paper listed here. Earn it in the narrative. Do not attribute it.

## Russia, China, the ledger

Not fetched. The outline's claims, and the status the draft must give them:

- Price liberalisation on 2 January 1992, under Yegor Gaidar. Standard date. Confirm in Gaidar before a scene is built on the morning.
- Viktor Gerashchenko's central bank, and the rouble zone, as the reason stabilisation failed while liberalisation proceeded. Sachs's later claim that the Polish monetary anchor and the stabilisation fund were both missing is his claim. Read him, and read a critic of that claim, before the chapter adopts his brief or sneers at it.
- Vouchers, 1992–94, and loans-for-shares, 1995–96, are different episodes. The second created the oligarchs. Do not blend them with 2 January 1992.
- Official output fell on the order of two-fifths or worse to the 1998 trough. **†** Shleifer and Treisman, "A Normal Country," argue the official series overstate the fall. Their PDF did not load. Until it does, give a range and name the dispute.
- Male life expectancy fell sharply in the early 1990s, concentrated on working-age men. **†** Cause disputed. PubMed (Notzon and co-authors) did not load. No year, no decimal, no verb of the form "shock therapy killed."
- China: household responsibility from the late 1970s; dual-track pricing in industry from the mid-1980s; no mass privatisation of state industry in the 1980s; political continuity after 1989. Confirm the 1984 date for the urban dual-track before being precise. The limits on the comparison — agrarian starting point, no CMEA shock, Hong Kong, a party that need not win an election — are the article's argument, and they should be stated as argument.
- EBRD transition indicators measure policy adoption and are then used to explain outcomes. That circularity is the outline's methodological remark. One dry sentence in the ledger chapter is enough, and only after an EBRD report is actually open.

The 2008 paragraph in particular must stay understated, and must not acquire figures that were not looked up.
